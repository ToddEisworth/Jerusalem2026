    1.2 An illustrative example

    Introduction

    The applications of pcf theory to cardinal arithmetic exploit a connection between two different versions of cofinality, one concerned with families of sets ordered by inclusion (such as the cofinality of \([\aleph_\omega]^{\aleph_0}\) ordered by \(\subseteq\)) and the other concerned with functions in infinite products ordered modulo an ideal on the index set (such as the cofinality of \(\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n\) modulo the finite ideal).

    The idea is that questions about the cofinalities of families of sets can sometimes be translated into questions about cofinalities of reduced products, and in this latter arena the tools of pcf theory can be used to analyze the situation. With luck, the pcf analysis then sheds light on the original question.

    This interaction is evident in the most famous theorem of pcf theory: Shelah's result on \(\aleph_{\omega_4}\). If we strip away conventional cardinal arithmetic and look at the pcf-theoretic heart of his result, we see there are two pieces:

    1. \(\operatorname{cf}\left([\aleph_\omega]^{\aleph_0}, \subseteq\right)=\max\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_{n+1}:n<\omega\})\), and
    2. \(\max\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_{n+1}:n<\omega\})<\aleph_{\omega_4}.\)

    The second statement is the part that grabs peoples' attention because of the mysterious appearance of \(\omega_4\), but our notes are going to focus on a general version of the first statement: When can questions about covering families of sets be translated into questions about reduced products of regular cardinals? This is the heart of the cov vs. pp problem.

    The example of \(\aleph_\omega\).

    To kick things off, we will sketch a proof of statement 1.

    Theorem

    \[\max\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_{n+1}:n<\omega\}) = \operatorname{cf}\left([\aleph_\omega]^{\aleph_0},\subseteq\right).\]

    Let \(\tau=\max\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_{n+1}:n<\omega\})\) and let \(\eta=\operatorname{cf}\left([\aleph_\omega]^{\aleph_0},\subseteq\right)\).

    One direction

    The inequality \(\tau\leq\eta\) is the easier of the two, so we dispose of it first. This part of the proof is quite flexible, and shows us how to pass from covering families to cofinal sets of functions.

    Given a countable subset \(X\) of \(\aleph_\omega\), we define the function \(g_X\in\prod A\) by

    \[ g_X(a)=\sup(X\cap a). \]

    Now suppose \(\mathcal{X}\) is cofinal in \(([\aleph_\omega]^{\aleph_0},\subseteq)\). Given any function \(f\in \prod A\), there will be an \(X\in \mathcal{X}\) such that

    \[ \{f(a)+1:a\in A\}\subseteq X. \]

    But then

    \[ a\in A\Longrightarrow f(a)< g_X(a), \]

    and so the family \(\{g_X:X\in\mathcal{X}\}\) is (pointwise) cofinal in \(\prod A\). Since \(\max\operatorname{pcf}(A)\) is the minimum cardinality of a pointwise cofinal subset of \(\prod A\), we see \(\tau\leq\eta\). We will encounter variants of this argument a little later as this is a basic tool.

    The Other Direction

    The reverse inequality is more difficult, and proving it here will provide us with a case study of another basic technique that involves internally approachable elementary submodels and their characteristic functions. This part of the argument is where we will use the fact that \(|A|<\omega_1<\min(A)\), as \(\omega_1\) will be the length of the chain of models we use. This may be a good time for the reader to take a look at Section 5 of Abraham–Magidor, Cardinal Arithmetic to remind themselves of the notation that we are borrowing.

    Fix a sufficiently large regular \(\chi\) and work in \(\mathfrak A=(H(\chi),\in,<_\chi)\), with the Skolem functions given by the fixed well-order \(<_\chi\). For each \(\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)\), fix a universal sequence \(\vec f^\eta=\langle f^\eta_\xi:\xi<\eta\rangle\) of functions in \(\prod A\) that is minimally obedient at cofinality \(\omega_1\). Let \(\vec f=\langle\vec f^\eta:\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)\rangle\) be the resulting array.

    Let \(Y\subseteq\lambda\) be countable. Choose an increasing continuous chain \(\langle N_i:i<\omega_1\rangle\) of countable elementary submodels of \(\mathfrak A\) with \(Y\subseteq N_0\) and \(\lambda,A,\omega_1,\vec f\in N_0\). Since \(A\) is countable, \(A\subseteq N_0\).

    At each successor stage arrange that \(\langle N_j:j\leq i\rangle\in N_{i+1}\). In particular, \(i,N_i\in N_{i+1}\). The union \(N=\bigcup_{i<\omega_1}N_i\) is internally approachable of length \(\omega_1\), has size \(\aleph_1\), and contains \(\omega_1+1\) pointwise. Its characteristic function on \(A\) is defined by

    \[ \operatorname{Ch}_N(\rho)=\sup(N\cap\rho)\qquad(\rho\in A). \]

    For each \(\rho\in A\), the sequence \(\langle\sup(N_i\cap\rho):i<\omega_1\rangle\) is continuous and strictly increasing. Indeed, \(\sup(N_i\cap\rho)\) and its successor both belong to \(N_{i+1}\cap\rho\). The sequence is cofinal in \(\operatorname{Ch}_N(\rho)\). Consequently \(N\cap\rho\) contains a club in \(\operatorname{Ch}_N(\rho)\), and this ordinal has cofinality \(\omega_1\).

    Model comparison

    We formulate the model comparison lemma for an arbitrary uncountable cardinal \(\lambda\) so that we can quote it later.

    Model Comparison Lemma

    Let \(\lambda\) be an uncountable cardinal and suppose \(M\) and \(N\) are elementary submodels of \(\mathfrak A\) with

    \[ M\subseteq N \text{ and }\lambda\in M. \]

    Let \(\eta<\lambda\) be an infinite cardinal, and assume

    1. \(M\cap\eta=N\cap\eta\).
    2. \(\sup(M\cap\rho)=\sup(N\cap\rho)\) for every regular cardinal \(\rho\in M\) with \(\eta<\rho\leq\lambda\).

    Then \(M\cap\lambda=N\cap\lambda\).

    Proof. Suppose \(\alpha\in(N\cap\lambda)\setminus M\). Since \(\lambda\in M\), we can choose a set \(X\in M\) of least possible cardinality such that \(\alpha\in X\subseteq\lambda\). Put \(\kappa=|X|\).

    If \(\kappa\leq\eta\), take a bijection \(b\colon\kappa\to X\) in \(M\). Then \(b^{-1}(\alpha)\in N\cap\kappa\subseteq M\) by (1), so \(\alpha\in M\), a contradiction.

    Put \(\rho=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\). By elementarity, \(\rho\in M\), and there is an increasing sequence \(\langle X_i:i<\rho\rangle\in M\) whose union is \(X\), with \(|X_i|<\kappa\) for every \(i<\rho\). By elementarity of \(N\), some \(i\in N\cap\rho\) satisfies \(\alpha\in X_i\).

    If \(\rho\leq\eta\), take \(j=i\in M\) by (1). Otherwise, \(\rho\) is a regular cardinal in \(M\) with \(\eta<\rho\leq\lambda\), so (2) gives \(j\in M\cap\rho\) with \(i\leq j\). In either case, \(X_j\in M\) contains \(\alpha\) and has size less than \(\kappa\), contradicting the choice of \(X\). \(\square\)

    Back to our proof

    Return to \(\lambda=\aleph_\omega\) and \(A=\{\aleph_n:2\leq n<\omega\}\). We use the Model Comparison Lemma to show that for an internally approachable model \(N\) as discussed above, the set \(N\cap\lambda\) is determined by the characteristic function of \(N\) on \(A\). Suppose \(N\) and \(N'\) are unions of towers as above with the same characteristic function on \(A\), and define \(M=N\cap N'\). Since the two models are closed under the same Skolem functions, \(M\prec\mathfrak A\).

    Take \(\eta=\omega_1\). All three models contain \(\omega_1\) pointwise, and for each \(\rho\in A\) the traces \(N\cap\rho\) and \(N'\cap\rho\) contain clubs in their common supremum, which has cofinality \(\omega_1\). These clubs intersect cofinally, so

    \[ \sup(M\cap\rho)=\sup(N\cap\rho)=\sup(N'\cap\rho). \]

    The regular cardinals in \((\omega_1,\lambda]\) are exactly the members of \(A\). Apply the Model Comparison Lemma to \(M\subseteq N\) and to \(M\subseteq N'\). It follows that \(N\cap\lambda=N'\cap\lambda\).

    The tower makes \(N\) \(\omega_1\)-presentable in the sense of Abraham–Magidor. By their Corollary 5.9, \(\operatorname{Ch}_N\) is the pointwise maximum of finitely many functions from the fixed array \(\vec f\). Since the array contains at most \(\max\operatorname{pcf}(A)\) functions, at most \(\max\operatorname{pcf}(A)\) characteristic functions occur. (See Abraham–Magidor, Cardinal Arithmetic, Definition 5.6 and Corollary 5.9.)

    We can now choose one representative tower for each characteristic function that occurs. Every countable \(Y\subseteq\lambda\) is contained in the trace of the union of one of the representative towers. By regularity of \(\omega_1\), \(Y\) is already contained in the trace of a countable stage of that tower. Taking all the countable stages of the representative towers therefore gives a cofinal family in \(([\lambda]^{\aleph_0},\subseteq)\) of size at most \(\max\operatorname{pcf}(A)\cdot\aleph_1=\max\operatorname{pcf}(A)\).
