    1.6 A Counting Lemma

    Reduction of covering numbers to singular cardinals

    The following theorem is proved by elementary combinatorics. It shows us how general covering numbers are governed by their values when the first two components are the same singular cardinal.

    Theorem

    Assume \(\lambda>\kappa\ge\theta>\sigma\), where \(\kappa\) is infinite and \(\sigma\) is either an infinite regular cardinal or \(2\). Then

    \[ \operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma) = \lambda+ \sup_{\substack{\kappa\le\nu\le\lambda\\ \sigma\le\operatorname{cf}(\nu)<\theta}} \operatorname{cov}(\nu,\nu,\theta,\sigma). \]

    Note that when computing \(\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)\) using the above formula, the right side involves all singular cardinals from the interval \([\kappa,\lambda]\) whose cofinality is from the interval \([\sigma,\theta)\). (The supremum of the empty set is understood to be \(0\).)

    Proof

    Let \(\chi\) be the right side. First observe that

    \[ \operatorname{cov}(\mu,\mu,\theta,\sigma)\le\chi \qquad(\kappa\le\mu\le\lambda). \tag{1} \]

    For the relevant singular \(\mu\), this follows from the definition of \(\chi\). For the other \(\mu\), use a cofinal sequence of proper initial segments of \(\mu\). If \(\operatorname{cf}(\mu)\ge\theta\), every subset of size \(<\theta\) is contained in one of these initial segments. If \(\operatorname{cf}(\mu)<\sigma\), the entire sequence has length \(<\sigma\) and covers all of \(\mu\). Thus these other diagonal covering numbers are at most \(\mu\le\chi\).

    We now prove by induction on cardinals \(\mu\le\lambda\) that there is a family

    \[ \mathcal P_\mu\subseteq[\mu]^{<\kappa}, \qquad |\mathcal P_\mu|\le\chi, \]

    such that every \(x\in[\mu]^{<\theta}\) is covered by fewer than \(\sigma\) members of \(\mathcal P_\mu\).

    For \(\mu<\kappa\), take \(\mathcal P_\mu=\{\mu\}\). For \(\mu\ge\kappa\), choose by (1) a family

    \[ \mathcal D_\mu\subseteq[\mu]^{<\mu}, \qquad |\mathcal D_\mu|\le\chi, \]

    witnessing the diagonal covering number. For each \(a\in\mathcal D_\mu\), the induction hypothesis, transported along a bijection between \(|a|\) and \(a\), gives a family

    \[ \mathcal P_a\subseteq[a]^{<\kappa}, \qquad |\mathcal P_a|\le\chi, \]

    covering every subset of \(a\) of size \(<\theta\) by fewer than \(\sigma\) members. Set

    \[ \mathcal P_\mu =\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal D_\mu}\mathcal P_a. \]

    Its size is at most \(\chi\).

    Given \(x\in[\mu]^{<\theta}\), first cover \(x\) by \(<\sigma\) members \(a\) of \(\mathcal D_\mu\), and then cover each \(x\cap a\) by \(<\sigma\) members of \(\mathcal P_a\). Regularity of \(\sigma\), or \(\sigma=2\), ensures that altogether we used \(<\sigma\) members of \(\mathcal P_\mu\). This completes the induction and proves the upper bound.

    Conversely, restricting any witness for \(\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)\) to \(\nu\) gives

    \[ \operatorname{cov}(\nu,\nu,\theta,\sigma) \le \operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma) \qquad(\kappa\le\nu\le\lambda). \]

    Also, because \(\kappa<\lambda\), fewer than \(\lambda\) sets of size \(<\kappa\) cannot cover all the singletons of \(\lambda\). Hence the covering number is at least \(\lambda\), proving equality.

    \[\tag*{$\square$}\]
