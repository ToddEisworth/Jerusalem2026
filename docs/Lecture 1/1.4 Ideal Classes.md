# 1.4 General Representability

There are a few natural ways to modify the definition of representability.  We will look at a couple of them here.


## $\theta$-representability 

Let $\lambda$ be singular and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda$. A regular cardinal $\chi>\lambda$ is **$\theta$-representable at $\lambda$** if there are a progressive set $A\subseteq\lambda\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ with $|A|\leq\theta$ and $\sup A=\lambda$, and a proper ideal $J\supseteq J_A^{\mathrm{bd}}$, such that $\chi=\operatorname{tcf}(\prod A/J)$. Define

$$\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda)=\sup\{\chi:\chi\text{ is }\theta\text{-representable at }\lambda\}.$$

Thus $\operatorname{pp}(\lambda)=\operatorname{pp}_{\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)}(\lambda)$.

Every $\theta$-representation is also a $\theta'$-representation whenever $\theta\leq\theta'<\lambda$, so $\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{pp}_{\theta'}(\lambda)$. Other variants of this notation should be given the obvious meaning, for example,  we define

$$\operatorname{pp}_{<\lambda}(\lambda)=\sup\{\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda):\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda\}.$$


## $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$-representability


We can also consider imposing more conditions on the ideals we use for representations.  For example, assume $\aleph_0\le\sigma=\operatorname{cf}(\sigma)\le\operatorname{cf}(\mu)<\theta<\mu$ and we define  $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$ to be the class of proper $\sigma$-complete ideals on sets of cardinality $<\theta$.

Given this, it makes sense to define $\operatorname{PP}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)$ consists of all regular cardinals $\kappa$ such that

$$\kappa=\operatorname{tcf}\left(\prod A/J\right),$$

where

$$A\subseteq\lambda\cap\operatorname{Reg},\qquad \sup A=\lambda,\qquad |A|<\theta,$$

and $J\in\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$ is an ideal on $A$ extending $J_A^{\mathrm{bd}}$.  Thus, $\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)$ is the supremum of all of the $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$-representable cardinals at $\lambda$.


The restrictions on $\sigma$ and $\theta$ are natural: if $\theta\leq\operatorname{\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)}$ then any set of cardinality $<\theta$ will be bounded, and we must require $\sigma\leq\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$ because $\lambda$ is a union of $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$ many bounded sets.

Note that $\operatorname{pp}_{\theta}(\lambda)=\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta^+,\aleph_0)}(\lambda)$ in this notation, so we have introduced a more general setting.

Also note a scale of length $\lambda^+$ at $\lambda$ shows us that $\lambda^+$ is ALWAYS $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$-representable for any relevant $\sigma$ and $\theta$, as the bounded ideal is $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$-complete and the support of the scale has cardinality $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$.  Thus

$$\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)\geq\lambda^+.$$


Finally, we have the following version of our characterization of pseudopowers in terms of pcf on tails.  It is essentially the same proof, just keeping track of the way the representations are built.


!!! lemma
    Suppose $\lambda$ is singular and $\aleph_0\leq\sigma\leq\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)<\theta<\lambda$, with $\sigma$ and $\theta$ regular. There is a $\beta$ with $\theta\leq\beta<\lambda$ such that every nonempty $B\subseteq(\beta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ of size less than $\theta$ satisfies

    $$
    \sup\operatorname{pcf}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(B)\leq \operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda).
    $$
    
    Equivalently, each such $B$ is the union of fewer than $\sigma$ nonempty sets $B_\alpha$ with $\max\operatorname{pcf}(B_\alpha)\leq \operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)$.

**Proof.** We use two elementary facts about a progressive set $A$. For a cardinal $\kappa$, if $\operatorname{tcf}(\prod A/J)=\nu>\kappa$, then $J_{\leq\kappa}[A]\subseteq J$.
 Conversely, if $I$ is a proper ideal containing $J_{\leq\kappa}[A]$, choose an $I$-positive $X\subseteq A$ with $\eta=\max\operatorname{pcf}(X)$ as small as possible. Then $\eta>\kappa$, and minimality gives $J_{<\eta}[X]\subseteq I\restriction X$. Hence the usual $\eta$-scale on $X$ remains a scale modulo $I\restriction X$.

Now proceed as in the ordinary case. If no cutoff works, choose counterexamples $B_i$ above cofinally increasing cutoffs greater than $\theta$, for $i<\kappa=\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$. Let $J_i$ be a proper $\sigma$-complete ideal on $B_i$ witnessing a true cofinality above $P$. The union $C=\bigcup_{i<\kappa}B_i$ has size less than $\theta$. Define an ideal $I$ on $C$ by
$$
X\in I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{i<\kappa:X\cap B_i\notin J_i\}\text{ is bounded in }\kappa.
$$
Then $I$ is proper and $\sigma$-complete, contains the bounded subsets of $C$, and contains $J_{<P^+}[C]$ by the first fact. The second fact now gives a $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$-representation at $\lambda$ above $P$, a contradiction.

For the equivalent formulation, apply the second fact to the $\sigma$-completion of $J_{<P^+}[B]$. If that completion were proper, it would give a $\sigma$-complete true cofinality above $P$ on a subset of $B$. Thus the completion contains $B$, which is exactly the asserted union property. The first fact gives the converse. $\square$
