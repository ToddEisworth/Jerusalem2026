# 1.5 Covering numbers

### Introduction

Covering numbers are a combinatorial tool for analyzing the structure of $([\lambda]^{<\lambda},\subseteq)$ when $\lambda$ is a cardinal.


!!! Definition
    The covering number $\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)$ is the least size of a family

    $$\mathcal{X}\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\kappa}$$

    such that every $a\in[\lambda]^{<\theta}$ is contained in the union of fewer than $\sigma$ members of $\mathcal{X}$.

### Easy facts

For fixed values of the other parameters,

- $\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)$ is weakly decreasing as $\kappa$ or $\sigma$ increases;
- $\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)$ is weakly increasing as $\lambda$ or $\theta$ increases.

### Identities and a pcf bound

Two easy observations:

$$\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\aleph_0)=\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,2).$$

$$\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa^+,\kappa^+,2)=\operatorname{cof}\bigl([\lambda]^\kappa,\subseteq\bigr).$$

### A connection to pseudopowers

The following result is a little deeper than the preceding observations. It tells us that covering numbers bound pseudopowers:


!!! Theorem
    If $\aleph_0\leq\sigma\leq\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)<\theta<\lambda$, then

    $$\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta,\sigma).$$

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{X}\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\lambda}$ witness the covering number. Suppose that $\chi$ is $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$-representable at $\lambda$, witnessed by a set $A\subseteq\lambda\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ with $|A|<\theta$ and $\sup A=\lambda$, and a proper $\sigma$-complete ideal $J\supseteq J_A^{\mathrm{bd}}$, so that

$$\chi=\operatorname{tcf}\left(\prod A/J\right).$$

We show that $\chi\leq|\mathcal{X}|$. Otherwise, for each $x\in\mathcal{X}$ define $g_x\in\prod A$ by

$$g_x(a)=\begin{cases}\sup(x\cap a),&|x|<a,\\0,&a\leq|x|.\end{cases}$$

Regularity of $a$ ensures that $g_x(a)<a$. Since $|\mathcal{X}|<\chi$, a scale of length $\chi$ gives a function $f\in\prod A$ such that $g_x<_J f$ for every $x\in\mathcal{X}$.

Now $|\operatorname{ran}(f)|<\theta$, so there are $\rho<\sigma$ and $x_i\in\mathcal{X}$ for $i<\rho$ such that

$$\operatorname{ran}(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{i<\rho}x_i.$$

For each $i<\rho$, let $B_i=\{a\in A:f(a)\in x_i\}$. If $a\in B_i$ and $a>|x_i|$, then $f(a)\leq g_{x_i}(a)$. Consequently,

$$B_i\subseteq\bigl(A\cap(|x_i|+1)\bigr)\cup\{a\in A:f(a)\leq g_{x_i}(a)\}\in J.$$

But $A=\bigcup_{i<\rho}B_i$, contradicting the $\sigma$-completeness and properness of $J$. Thus $\chi\leq|\mathcal{X}|$, and taking the supremum over all such representations proves the inequality. $\square$
