# 1.7 The cov vs. pp Theorem

Can we go the other direction and use pseudopowers to bound covering numbers?  The answer is "yes" in many cases, and we'll take a look at what is known.
We are going to start with Shelah's cov vs. pp theorem, which tells us that covering numbers and pseudopowers are closely related, and we have an exact match between the two provided we are working with countably complete covers and ideals.


### The cov vs. pp theorem (Singular Cardinals Version)

!!! Theorem
    If $\aleph_0<\sigma=\operatorname{cf}(\sigma)\leq\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)<\theta<\lambda$, then

    $$\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\lambda)=\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta,\sigma).$$

### The general cov vs. pp theorem

If we combine the above result with Lemma ? we obtain the following more general form of the cov vs pp theorem.

!!! Theorem
    If $\aleph_0<\sigma=\operatorname{cf}(\sigma)\leq\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)<\theta<\lambda$ with $\theta\leq\kappa\leq\lambda$ then

    $$\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\kappa,\theta,\sigma)+\lambda=\sup_{\substack{\kappa\leq\mu\leq\lambda\\\sigma\leq\operatorname{cf}(\mu)<\theta}}\operatorname{pp}_{\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)}(\mu)+\lambda.$$

If $\kappa<\lambda$, then the $+\lambda$ on the left can be omitted.  We want to note how the parameters from the left side appear on the right:  we are covering sets in $[\lambda]^{<\theta}$ with unions of $<\sigma$ sets from a family $\mathcal{P}\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\kappa}$.  On the right side, $\theta$ and $\sigma$ appear in the types of
representations we are allowing, while the supremum is taken over those singular cardinals in the interval $[\kappa,\lambda]$ for which representations using ideals from  $\Gamma(\theta,\sigma)$ makes sense.


The restriction to uncountable $\sigma$ is unfortunate, because it means the above theorems shed no light on what occurs at cardinals of countable cofinality in general.
