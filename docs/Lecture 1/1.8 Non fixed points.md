# 1.8 Non fixed points

We will now take a look at a result of Shelah that shows us how we pcf theory can be deployed more effectively when dealing with singular cardinals $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$.


!!! theorem
    Suppose $\lambda$ is singular, $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda$. Then

    $$\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta^+,2)=\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda).$$
    
    In particular,

    $$\operatorname{cov}\bigl(\lambda,\lambda,\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)^+,2\bigr)=\operatorname{pp}(\lambda).$$

This result is from *Cardinal Arithmetic*, Chapter IX, Claim 3.7, and *Cardinal arithmetic for skeptics*, Theorem 5.7. We have tried to give a more straightforward presentation of the proof.


### What's special about a non fixed point?

The proof requires a little more pcf theory than we've assumed elsewhere.  To start,  we need the following (somewhat famous) result of Shelah that we quote as a black box.

!!! theorem 
    If $A$ is a progressive interval of regular cardinals, then $|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<|A|^{+4}$.


The above theorem is crucial for us because we are working with singular $\lambda$ such that $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, because for any such $\lambda$ there is a $\delta<\lambda$ so that $(\delta,\lambda)\cap\mathsf{Reg}$ is a progressive interval of regular cardinals.  The next easy lemma uses this fact.



!!! lemma

    Suppose $\lambda$ is singular, $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda$. There is a regular cardinal $\delta$ with $\theta<\delta<\lambda$ such that, for $A=(\delta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$, we have

    1. $|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<\delta$, and
    2. $\max\operatorname{pcf}(B)\leq\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda)$ for every nonempty $B\subseteq A$ of size at most $\theta$.

**Proof.** As we observed earlier, we can choose $\beta<\lambda$ so that (2) holds for every nonempty $B\subseteq(\beta,\lambda)\cap\mathsf{Reg}$ of size at most $\theta$.

Write $\lambda=\aleph_\alpha$. Since $\lambda$ is not a fixed point, $\alpha<\lambda$. Put $\kappa=|\alpha|$. As $\lambda$ is a limit cardinal, $\kappa^{+4}<\lambda$, so we may choose a regular cardinal $\delta$ with

$$\max\{\beta,\theta,\kappa^{+4}\}<\delta<\lambda.$$

Now $A=(\delta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ has size at most $\kappa$, since every cardinal in $A$ has index below $\alpha$. Thus $|A|\leq\kappa<\delta<\min A$, and Shelah's bound for progressive intervals of regular cardinals gives

$$|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<|A|^{+4}\leq\kappa^{+4}<\delta.$$

This proves (1), and (2) follows from $\beta<\delta$. $\square$


### More pcf theory

For a progressive set $A$ of regular cardinals, we know we can fix generators  

$$\langle \mathsf{b}_\eta[A]:\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)\rangle,$$

which means 

$$J_{\leq\eta}[A]=J_{<\eta}[A]+\mathsf{b}_\eta[A].$$

Note that with this setup, every set in $J_{<\eta}[A]$ is covered by finitely many generators $\mathsf{b}_\xi[A]$ with $\xi<\eta$.


Moreover, we know  for each $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}[A]$ that

$$\eta = \operatorname{tcf}\left(\prod \mathsf{b}_\eta[A]/ J_{<\eta}[A]\right)$$

and so we can fix a scale


$$\langle f^\eta_\xi:\xi<\eta\rangle$$

in $\prod b_\eta/J_{<\eta}[A]$, and can extend these functions by zero to the rest of $A$. 

We can also arrange things so that the scale is continuous: at a limit stage, if the preceding sequence has an exact upper bound in $\prod \mathsf{b}_\eta$, use such an exact upper bound as the next value.



## Two elementary-model lemmas

Fix a sufficiently large regular $\Omega$, a well-order of $H(\Omega)$, and the associated definable Skolem functions. All models and hulls below use this same expanded structure, denoted by $\mathfrak H$.

### Lemma (Characteristic functions)

Suppose $A$ is progressive, $\tau$ is uncountable and regular, and

$$|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<\tau<\min A.$$

Let $\langle N_i:i\leq\tau\rangle$ be an increasing continuous chain of elementary submodels of $\mathfrak H$ such that, for $i<\tau$,

$$|N_i|<\tau,\qquad\langle N_j:j\leq i\rangle\in N_{i+1}.$$

Assume that $N_0$ contains $A$, the generator and continuous-scale systems, and every member of $\operatorname{pcf}(A)$. Put $N=N_\tau$ and

$$h(a)=\sup(N\cap a),\qquad\gamma_\eta=\sup(N\cap\eta).$$

Then

$$h\restriction b_\eta=_{J_{<\eta}[A]}f^\eta_{\gamma_\eta}\restriction b_\eta\qquad(\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)).$$

Also, for each $a\in A$, $\operatorname{cf}(h(a))=\tau$, and $N\cap a$ contains a club in $h(a)$.

### Proof

For $i<\tau$, put $h_i(a)=\sup(N_i\cap a)$. Since $N_i$ and $a$ belong to $N_{i+1}$, we have $h_i(a)\in N_{i+1}\cap a$. Thus $\langle h_i(a):i<\tau\rangle$ is strictly increasing and continuous, with supremum $h(a)$. Its range is a club in $h(a)$ contained in $N$, and $\operatorname{cf}(h(a))=\tau$. The same argument gives $\gamma_\eta<\eta$ and $\operatorname{cf}(\gamma_\eta)=\tau$.

Fix $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)$. If $\xi\in N\cap\eta$, then all values of $f^\eta_\xi$ on $b_\eta$ belong to $N$, so they are strictly below $h$. Since $N\cap\eta$ is cofinal in $\gamma_\eta$, it follows that $h\restriction b_\eta$ bounds the entire scale below $\gamma_\eta$.

We verify exactness. Suppose $g<h\restriction b_\eta$ pointwise. Since $|b_\eta|<\tau$, one $i<\tau$ satisfies

$$g(a)<h_i(a)\qquad(a\in b_\eta).$$

The function $h_i$ belongs to $N_{i+1}$. By elementarity and cofinality of the scale, there is $\xi\in N_{i+1}\cap\eta$ such that

$$h_i\restriction b_\eta<_{J_{<\eta}[A]}f^\eta_\xi\restriction b_\eta.$$

Thus $g$ lies below a scale value indexed below $\gamma_\eta$. If $g$ is only below $h$ modulo the ideal, first replace its values by zero on the exceptional set.

Therefore $h\restriction b_\eta$ is an exact upper bound for the scale below $\gamma_\eta$. Continuity of the scale and uniqueness of exact upper bounds modulo the ideal give the required equality. $\square$

### Lemma (Model comparison)

Suppose $M,L\prec\mathfrak H$ contain a singular cardinal $\mu$. Let $\nu<\mu$ be an infinite cardinal with $\nu+1\subseteq L$. Suppose that, for every regular cardinal $a\in M$ with $\nu<a<\mu$,

$$M\cap L\cap a\text{ is cofinal in }M\cap a.$$

Then $M\cap\mu\subseteq L$.

### Proof

Otherwise choose $X\in M\cap L$, $X\subseteq\mu$, of least cardinality such that $M\cap X\nsubseteq L$. Such an $X$ exists because $X=\mu$ works.

Put $\kappa=|X|$, and use the common definable bijection from $\kappa$ onto $X$ to obtain

$$\xi\in M\cap\kappa\setminus L.$$

Necessarily $\kappa>\nu$.

If $\kappa$ is singular, choose a common increasing cofinal map $e:\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\longrightarrow\kappa$. By minimality,

$$M\cap\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\subseteq L.$$

Choose $i\in M\cap\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ with $\xi<e(i)$. Then $e(i)\in M\cap L$, and the ordinal $e(i)$ is a smaller counterexample.

If $\kappa$ is regular, then $\kappa<\mu$, since $\mu$ is singular. By the cofinality assumption, choose $\zeta\in M\cap L\cap\kappa$ with $\xi<\zeta$. Again, the ordinal $\zeta$ is a smaller counterexample. $\square$

## Proof of the theorem

Put $P=\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\mu)$.

### The easy inequality

We first recall why

$$P\leq\operatorname{cov}(\mu,\mu,\theta^+,2).$$

Let $\chi$ be represented at $\mu$ on a progressive set $B$ of size at most $\theta$, modulo a proper ideal $J$ containing the bounded subsets of $B$. Suppose $\mathcal X\subseteq[\mu]^{<\mu}$ is a covering family of size less than $\chi$.

For each $x\in\mathcal X$, define

$$g_x(a)=\begin{cases}\sup(x\cap a),&|x|<a,\\0,&a\leq|x|.\end{cases}$$

Regularity gives $g_x(a)<a$. A scale of length $\chi$ supplies $f\in\prod B$ with $g_x<_J f$ for every $x\in\mathcal X$.

Choose $x\in\mathcal X$ containing $\operatorname{ran}(f)$. For every $a\in B$ above $|x|$, we have $f(a)\leq g_x(a)$, contradicting $g_x<_J f$. Taking the supremum over representations proves the inequality.

### Fixing the parameters

Use the interval-pcf estimate and the tail lemma to choose

$$A=(\beta,\mu)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$$

and an uncountable regular $\tau$ such that

$$\max\{\theta,|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|\}<\tau<\min A,$$

and every nonempty $B\subseteq A$ of size at most $\theta$ satisfies $\max\operatorname{pcf}(B)\leq P$.

Fix generators and continuous scales on $A$. For every limit $\gamma<\mu$, fix a club $e_\gamma\subseteq\gamma$ of order type $\operatorname{cf}(\gamma)$.

Let $q$ collect these parameters, including $\mu$, $\theta$, $\tau$, and $A$. Fix an infinite cardinal $\nu<\mu$ with

$$\beta,\tau,|A|\leq\nu.$$

The covering family will consist of traces of hulls

$$L_g=\operatorname{Hull}\bigl((\nu+1)\cup\{q,g\}\bigr),$$

where $g$ ranges over at most $P$ finite codes. Every such hull has size $\nu<\mu$.

### Choosing the models

Fix $Y\in[\mu]^{\leq\theta}$. Build a chain $\langle N_i:i\leq\tau\rangle$ as in the characteristic-function lemma, with

$$Y\cup\operatorname{pcf}(A)\subseteq N_0,\qquad q\in N_0.$$

Put $N=N_\tau$, and define $h$ and $\gamma_\eta$ as in that lemma. For each $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)$, choose a finite set $w_\eta\subseteq\operatorname{pcf}(A)\cap\eta$ such that, on

$$d_\eta=b_\eta\setminus\bigcup_{\xi\in w_\eta}b_\xi,$$

we have

$$h\restriction d_\eta=f^\eta_{\gamma_\eta}\restriction d_\eta.$$

This is possible because the exceptional set belongs to $J_{<\eta}[A]$.

For every $a\in A$, the set $N\cap a$ contains a club in $h(a)$, and $\operatorname{cf}(h(a))=\tau>\omega$. Intersecting that club with $e_{h(a)}$ shows that

$$N\cap e_{h(a)}\text{ is cofinal in }h(a).$$

Choose an external function $F:N^2\longrightarrow N$ such that

$$x<F(a,x)\in N\cap e_{h(a)}\qquad(a\in A,\ x\in N\cap a).$$

Define $F$ arbitrarily on other pairs. By downward Löwenheim–Skolem in the expansion of $N$ by $F$, choose $M\prec N$ of size at most $\theta$ with

$$Y\subseteq M,\qquad q\in M,$$

and $M$ closed under $F$. Consequently,

$$M\cap e_{h(a)}\text{ is cofinal in }M\cap a\qquad(a\in M\cap A).$$

The function $F$ is used only to choose $M$. It will not be a parameter in the covering family.

### Encoding finitely many scale values

Put $B=M\cap A$. We claim that finitely many $d_\eta$, with $\eta\leq P$, cover $B$.

Start with $B_0=B$. If $B_n\neq\varnothing$, let

$$\eta_n=\max\operatorname{pcf}(B_n),\qquad B_{n+1}=B_n\setminus d_{\eta_n}.$$

The tail bound gives $\eta_n\leq P$. Since $B_n\in J_{\leq\eta_n}[A]$, the generator property gives

$$B_{n+1}\in J_{<\eta_n}[A].$$

Thus $\eta_{n+1}<\eta_n$ whenever $B_{n+1}$ is nonempty. There is no infinite strictly decreasing sequence of ordinals, so the process terminates.

We obtain a finite $u\subseteq\operatorname{pcf}(A)\cap(P+1)$ such that

$$B\subseteq D:=\bigcup_{\eta\in u}d_\eta.$$

Define

$$h^*=\bigcup_{\eta\in u}\left(f^\eta_{\gamma_\eta}\restriction d_\eta\right).$$

The pieces agree on overlaps, because each agrees with $h$. Thus $h^*=h\restriction D$ and $M\cap A\subseteq D$.

The code for $h^*$ consists of the finite data

$$u,\qquad\langle\gamma_\eta,w_\eta:\eta\in u\rangle.$$

Every ordinal in this code is at most $P$: $\eta\leq P$, $\gamma_\eta<\eta$, and $w_\eta$ is a finite subset of $\eta$. With the generator and scale systems fixed, there are therefore at most $P$ possible codes.

More explicitly, let $\mathcal G$ be the family of all partial functions obtained by finite unions

$$g=\bigcup_{\eta\in u}\left(f^\eta_{\xi_\eta}\restriction\left(b_\eta\setminus\bigcup_{\zeta\in w_\eta}b_\zeta\right)\right),$$

where $u\subseteq\operatorname{pcf}(A)\cap(P+1)$ is finite, $\xi_\eta<\eta$, $w_\eta\subseteq\operatorname{pcf}(A)\cap\eta$ is finite, and the pieces agree on overlaps. Then

$$|\mathcal G|\leq P,\qquad h^*\in\mathcal G.$$

This family is fixed independently of $Y$.

### Recovering common cofinal subsets

Let

$$L=\operatorname{Hull}\bigl((\nu+1)\cup\{q,h^*\}\bigr).$$

Then $|L|=\nu<\mu$. The set $D=\operatorname{dom}(h^*)$ belongs to $L$ and has size at most $\nu$, so $D\subseteq L$.

If $a\in M\cap A$, then $a\in D$, hence

$$h(a)=h^*(a)\in L.$$

It follows that $e_{h(a)}\in L$. This club has size $\tau\leq\nu$, so

$$e_{h(a)}\subseteq L.$$

Since $M\cap e_{h(a)}$ is cofinal in $M\cap a$, we conclude that

$$M\cap L\cap a\text{ is cofinal in }M\cap a\qquad(a\in M\cap A).$$

Every regular $a\in M$ with $\nu<a<\mu$ belongs to $A$. The model-comparison lemma therefore gives

$$Y\subseteq M\cap\mu\subseteq L\cap\mu.$$

### The covering family

Define

$$\mathcal X=\{\mu\cap L_g:g\in\mathcal G\}.$$

Each member has size at most $\nu<\mu$, and $|\mathcal X|\leq P$. The preceding argument shows that every $Y\in[\mu]^{\leq\theta}$ is contained in a member of $\mathcal X$. Hence

$$\operatorname{cov}(\mu,\mu,\theta^+,2)\leq P.$$

Together with the easy inequality, this proves the theorem. $\square$

## The uniform covering corollary

We now extract the form needed for the fixed-point argument.

### Lemma (Covering decomposition)

Suppose $\theta\leq\nu<\chi$ are infinite cardinals. Then

$$\operatorname{cov}(\chi,\nu^+,\theta^+,2)=\chi+\sup_{\substack{\nu<\rho\leq\chi\\\operatorname{cf}(\rho)\leq\theta}}\operatorname{cov}(\rho,\rho,\theta^+,2).$$

The supremum ranges over cardinals; an empty supremum is zero.

### Proof

Let $K$ be the right side. The left side is at least $\chi$, since the covering family must cover all singletons using sets of size at most $\nu<\chi$. Restricting a witnessing family to any $\rho>\nu$ also gives

$$\operatorname{cov}(\rho,\rho,\theta^+,2)\leq\operatorname{cov}(\chi,\nu^+,\theta^+,2).$$

This proves one inequality.

For the other, induct on cardinals $\rho\leq\chi$ to cover $[\rho]^{\leq\theta}$ by at most $K$ sets of size at most $\nu$. For $\rho\leq\nu$, one set suffices.

If $\operatorname{cf}(\rho)>\theta$, every relevant subset is bounded in $\rho$. Transfer the earlier covering families to all initial segments and take their union, using at most $\rho\cdot K=K$ sets.

If $\operatorname{cf}(\rho)\leq\theta$, choose a family of at most $K$ sets of size less than $\rho$ covering $[\rho]^{\leq\theta}$. Refine each of its members using the induction hypothesis. This uses at most $K\cdot K=K$ sets of size at most $\nu$. $\square$

### Corollary (An interval containing no fixed point)

Suppose $\theta\leq\nu<\chi$ and there is no $\aleph$-fixed point in $(\nu,\chi]$. Then

$$\operatorname{cov}(\chi,\nu^+,\theta^+,2)=\chi+\sup_{\substack{\nu<\rho\leq\chi\\\operatorname{cf}(\rho)\leq\theta}}\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\rho).$$

### Proof

Every $\rho$ in the supremum is singular and is not a fixed point. Apply the non-fixed-point theorem in the covering decomposition. $\square$

### Corollary (A non-fixed endpoint)

Suppose $\theta<\lambda<\chi$ and $\chi\neq\aleph_\chi$. Then some cardinal $\nu\in[\lambda,\chi)$ satisfies

$$\operatorname{cov}(\chi,\nu^+,\theta^+,2)=\chi+\sup_{\substack{\nu<\rho\leq\chi\\\operatorname{cf}(\rho)\leq\theta}}\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\rho).$$

### Proof

Write $\chi=\aleph_\alpha$, with $\alpha<\chi$, and choose

$$\max\{\lambda,|\alpha|\}\leq\nu<\chi.$$

If $\rho=\aleph_\rho\leq\chi=\aleph_\alpha$, then $\rho\leq\alpha$, hence $\rho\leq|\alpha|\leq\nu$. Thus $(\nu,\chi]$ contains no fixed point, and the preceding corollary applies. $\square$

There is no restriction on $\operatorname{cf}(\chi)$. When $\operatorname{cf}(\chi)\leq\theta$, the endpoint occurs in the supremum; when $\operatorname{cf}(\chi)>\theta$, only smaller cardinals occur.

### The bound used in the fixed-point proof

Suppose $\theta<\lambda\leq P$ and

$$\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\rho)\leq P\qquad\bigl(\lambda<\rho\leq P,\ \operatorname{cf}(\rho)\leq\theta\bigr).$$

Then every non-fixed cardinal $\chi$ with $\lambda<\chi\leq P$ admits a cardinal $\nu\in[\lambda,\chi)$ such that

$$\operatorname{cov}(\chi,\nu^+,\theta^+,2)\leq P.$$

This follows immediately from the preceding corollary.

In the later application, $\lambda$ is strong limit of countable cofinality, $\theta=\mathfrak c$, and $P=\operatorname{pp}(\lambda)$. Compression and inverse monotonicity give the required pseudopower bounds. The resulting covering family allows us to lower a putative non-fixed least covering cutoff.
