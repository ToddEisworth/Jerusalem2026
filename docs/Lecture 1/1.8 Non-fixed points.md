# 1.8 Non-fixed points

We will now take a look at a result of Shelah that shows us how we pcf theory can be deployed more effectively when dealing with singular cardinals $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$.


!!! theorem
    Suppose $\lambda$ is singular, $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda$. Then

    $$\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta^+,2)=\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda).$$
    
    In particular,

    $$\operatorname{cov}\bigl(\lambda,\lambda,\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)^+,2\bigr)=\operatorname{pp}(\lambda).$$

This result is from *Cardinal Arithmetic*, Chapter IX, Claim 3.7, and *Cardinal arithmetic for skeptics*, Theorem 5.7. We have tried to give a more straightforward presentation of the proof along the lines of Abraham and Magidor's handbook article.


### What's special about a non-fixed point?

The proof requires a little more pcf theory than we've assumed elsewhere.  To start,  we need the following (somewhat famous) result of Shelah that we quote as a black box.

!!! theorem 
    If $A$ is a progressive interval of regular cardinals, then $|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<|A|^{+4}$.


The above theorem is crucial for us because we are working with singular $\lambda$ such that $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, because for any such $\lambda$ there is a $\delta<\lambda$ so that $(\delta,\lambda)\cap\mathsf{Reg}$ is a progressive interval of regular cardinals.  The next easy lemma uses this fact.



!!! lemma

    Suppose $\lambda$ is singular, $\lambda<\aleph_\lambda$, and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)\leq\theta<\lambda$. There is a regular cardinal $\delta$ with $\theta<\delta<\lambda$ such that, for $A=(\delta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$, we have

    1. $|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<\delta$, and
    2. $\max\operatorname{pcf}(B)\leq\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda)$ for every nonempty $B\subseteq A$ of size at most $\theta$.

**Proof.** As we observed earlier, we can choose $\beta<\lambda$ so that (2) holds for every nonempty $B\subseteq(\beta,\lambda)\cap\mathsf{Reg}$ of size at most $\theta$.

Write $\lambda=\aleph_\alpha$. Since $\lambda$ is not a fixed point, $\alpha<\lambda$. Put $\kappa=|\alpha|$. Since $\lambda$ is a limit cardinal we know $\kappa^{+4}<\lambda$, and so we may choose a regular cardinal $\delta$ with

$$\max\{\beta,\theta,\kappa^{+4}\}<\delta<\lambda.$$

Now $A=(\delta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ has size at most $\kappa$, since every cardinal in $A$ has index below $\alpha$. Thus $|A|\leq\kappa<\delta<\min A$, and Shelah's bound for progressive intervals of regular cardinals gives

$$|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<|A|^{+4}\leq\kappa^{+4}<\delta.$$

This proves (1), and (2) follows from $\beta<\delta$. $\square$


Put $P=\operatorname{pp}_\theta(\lambda)$, and take $\delta$ and $A=(\delta,\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Reg}$ from the preceding lemma. Then

$$
|A|\leq|\operatorname{pcf}(A)|<\delta<\min A.
$$

Fix an array $\vec f=\langle\vec f^\eta:\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)\rangle$, where each $\vec f^\eta=\langle f^\eta_\xi:\xi<\eta\rangle$ is a universal sequence in $\prod A$ that is minimally obedient at cofinality $\delta$. The inequality $|A|<\delta<\min A$ allows us to choose these sequences. [Abraham–Magidor, *Cardinal Arithmetic*, §5.2, following Definition 5.6.](https://www.winterschool.eu/files/3-Cardinal_Arithmetic.pdf)

#### The lower bound

As in the $\aleph_\omega$ example, a covering family gives a cofinal family in every reduced product $\prod B/J$ used to compute $P$. For each covering set $X$, put $g_X(a)=\sup(X\cap a)$ when $a>|X|$ and $g_X(a)=0$ otherwise. Given $f\in\prod B$, choose $X$ containing $\{f(a)+1:a\in B\}$, a set of size at most $\theta$. Then $f(a)<g_X(a)$ on the tail above $|X|$, so $f<_J g_X$ since $J$ contains the bounded subsets of $B$. Taking the supremum over the representations gives $P\leq\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta^+,2)$.

#### Reading the characteristic function from the scales

For each $\gamma<\lambda$ of cofinality $\delta$, fix a club $e_\gamma$ in $\gamma$ of order type $\delta$. Work in one sufficiently large Skolemized structure $\mathfrak A$ on $H(\chi)$, with $\lambda,\delta,A,\vec f$, and the club sequence named.

Fix $Y\in[\lambda]^{\leq\theta}$. Build an increasing continuous chain $\langle N_i:i\leq\delta\rangle$ of elementary submodels with $|N_i|<\delta$ for $i<\delta$, with $Y\cup\operatorname{pcf}(A)\subseteq N_0$, and with $\langle N_j:j\leq i\rangle\in N_{i+1}$ for each $i<\delta$. Its union $N=N_\delta$ has size $\delta$ and contains $\delta+1$ pointwise, so it is $\delta$-presentable in the sense of Abraham–Magidor.

Put $h=\operatorname{Ch}_N\restriction A$ and $\gamma_\eta=\sup(N\cap\eta)$ for $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)$. As in the argument at $\aleph_\omega$, the stage suprema form a club in $h(a)$ contained in $N\cap a$, and $\operatorname{cf}(h(a))=\delta$ for each $a\in A$.

For each $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)$, both $\eta$ and $\vec f$ belong to $N_0$, so $\vec f^\eta\in N$. Also $\gamma_\eta<\eta$ because $\eta$ is regular and $|N|=\delta<\min A\leq\eta$. Abraham–Magidor then give $f^\eta_{\gamma_\eta}\leq h$ pointwise on $A$. Moreover, the agreement set

$$
C_\eta=\{a\in A:f^\eta_{\gamma_\eta}(a)=h(a)\}
$$

is a generator for $\eta$, so $J_{\leq\eta}[A]=J_{<\eta}[A]+C_\eta$. [Abraham–Magidor, *Cardinal Arithmetic*, §5.2, Lemmas 5.4, 5.7, and 5.8(1).](https://www.winterschool.eu/files/3-Cardinal_Arithmetic.pdf)

#### Coding the coordinates needed for $Y$

For each $a\in A$, the club in $h(a)$ contained in $N\cap a$ meets $e_{h(a)}$ cofinally. Fix a function on $N$ that, given $a\in A$ and $x\in N\cap a$, selects a point of $N\cap e_{h(a)}$ above $x$. Take $M\prec N$ of size at most $\theta$ containing $Y$ pointwise and closed under this function. Then

$$
M\cap e_{h(a)}\text{ is cofinal in }M\cap a
\qquad(a\in M\cap A).
$$

Abraham and Magidor's Corollary 5.9 expresses $h$ on all of $A$ as a finite pointwise maximum of functions from the array. For the model comparison below, we will find a finite pointwise maximum $g$ that agrees with $h$ on $M\cap A$ using only sequences of length at most $P$.

Put $B_0=M\cap A$. While $B_n$ is nonempty, put

$$
\eta_n=\max\operatorname{pcf}(B_n),
\qquad B_{n+1}=B_n\setminus C_{\eta_n}.
$$

Since $B_n\subseteq M\cap A$ has size at most $\theta$, the tail bound gives $\eta_n\leq P$. Since $B_n\in J_{\leq\eta_n}[A]$ and $C_{\eta_n}$ is a generator, $B_{n+1}\in J_{<\eta_n}[A]$. Thus $\eta_{n+1}<\eta_n$ whenever $B_{n+1}$ is nonempty, so the process terminates after finitely many steps.

Let $g$ be the pointwise maximum of the finitely many selected functions $f^{\eta_n}_{\gamma_{\eta_n}}$. Each is pointwise at most $h$, and at every coordinate in $M\cap A$ at least one of them equals $h$. Hence $g=h$ on $M\cap A$.

Let $\mathcal G$ consist of all finite pointwise maxima of functions $f^\eta_\xi$ with $\eta\in\operatorname{pcf}(A)$ and $\xi<\eta\leq P$. The finite list of pairs $(\eta_n,\gamma_{\eta_n})$ determines $g$ on all of $A$. There are at most $P$ possible pairs, and hence at most $P$ finite lists. Thus $g$ lies in a family $\mathcal G$ of size at most $P$ fixed independently of $Y$.

#### Applying model comparison

Let

$$
L=\operatorname{Hull}_{\mathfrak A}\bigl((\delta+1)\cup\{g\}\bigr),
\qquad K=M\cap L.
$$

The common Skolem functions give $K\prec\mathfrak A$, and $\delta\subseteq L$ gives $K\cap\delta=M\cap\delta$.

Let $a\in K$ be regular with $\delta<a\leq\lambda$. Since $\lambda$ is singular, $a\in A$, so $a\in M\cap A$ and $h(a)=g(a)\in L$. Hence $e_{h(a)}\in L$. Its increasing enumeration has domain $\delta$, so $\delta\subseteq L$ gives $e_{h(a)}\subseteq L$. Therefore $K\cap a$ contains $M\cap e_{h(a)}$, which is cofinal in $M\cap a$.

Apply the Model Comparison Lemma to $K\subseteq M$ with cutoff $\delta$. We obtain

$$
Y\subseteq M\cap\lambda=K\cap\lambda\subseteq L\cap\lambda.
$$

As $g$ ranges over $\mathcal G$, the traces of these hulls give at most $P$ sets of size at most $\delta$ covering $[\lambda]^{\leq\theta}$. Together with the lower bound, this yields

$$
P
=\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\lambda,\theta^+,2)
=\operatorname{cov}(\lambda,\delta^+,\theta^+,2).
$$
